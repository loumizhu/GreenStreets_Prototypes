# Technical Dossier Evidence Guide (Annex VII)
**The Substantive Technical Backbone Behind Every PPWR Declaration of Conformity**

---

## 1. Statutory Role of Annex VII (Module A)

Under **Article 15(2)** and **Annex VII** of Regulation (EU) 2025/40, the manufacturer must draw up technical documentation *before* issuing an EU Declaration of Conformity. 

> [!IMPORTANT]
> A Declaration of Conformity without a supporting Annex VII Technical Dossier is legally void. If market surveillance authorities request verification, the economic operator must produce the technical file within **10 working days** (Article 15(10)). Failure to provide it triggers formal non-compliance procedures under **Article 62(1)(d)** and potential market withdrawal under **Article 58 & Article 62(2)**.

## 2. Mandatory Technical File Sections & Evidence Requirements

### Section 1: General Packaging Specification & Bill of Materials (BOM)
The dossier must contain conceptual design drawings, dimensions, component breakdowns, and full material specifications.

#### 1.1 Structural BOM Hierarchy
Every packaging unit must be broken down into discrete components:
* **Primary (Sales) Packaging:** Direct contact container, closure/cap, liners, labels, neck rings, safety seals, tamper-evident strips.
* **Secondary (Grouped) Packaging:** Multi-pack wraps, collating trays, dividers, display outer boxes.
* **Tertiary (Transport) Packaging:** Pallet, stretch wrap film, plastic/steel strapping, edge protectors, slip sheets.

#### 1.2 Data Elements Required Per Component
1. Component name and functional role.
2. Material identification code pursuant to Commission Decision 97/129/EC (e.g., PAP 20, PAP 21, PET 01, HDPE 02, GL 70, ALU 41).
3. Tare weight in grams (accurate to ±0.1g for primary components, ±1g for shippers).
4. Percentage weight of the component relative to the total packaging unit.
5. Base polymer / substrate chemical breakdown (e.g., 94% Virgin Kraft Paper + 4% PVDC coating + 2% EVA adhesive).

### Section 2: Substances of Concern, Heavy Metals & PFAS Compliance (Article 5)

Article 5 sets rigorous chemical safety limits across all packaging formats:

#### 2.1 Heavy Metals Ceiling (Pb, Cd, Hg, Cr(VI))
* **Statutory Limit:** The sum of concentration levels of **lead, cadmium, mercury, and hexavalent chromium** shall not exceed **100 mg/kg (100 ppm)** by weight in any packaging or packaging component.
* **Evidentiary Standard:** 
  - Laboratory test reports following **EN 13428:2004** / **IEC 62321** using Inductively Coupled Plasma Mass Spectrometry (ICP-MS) or Optical Emission Spectrometry (ICP-OES).
  - Supplier raw material declarations alone are unacceptable unless backed by certified test reports conducted within the preceding 24 months.

#### 2.2 PFAS Ban in Food Contact Packaging (Article 5(5) – Applicable from 12 August 2026)
* **Statutory Prohibition:** Food-contact packaging placed on the EU market shall not contain **per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS)** equal to or above:
  * **25 ppb** for any individual targeted PFAS substance (polymeric PFAS excluded from quantification).
  * **250 ppb** for the sum of targeted PFAS substances, where applicable with prior degradation of precursors.
  * **50 ppm** for PFASs (including polymeric PFAS).
  * *Fluorine screening rule:* If total fluorine exceeds **50 mg/kg**, the manufacturer or importer must obtain proof of the quantity of fluorine measured as PFAS vs non-PFAS to include in the Annex VII technical documentation.
* **Evidentiary Standard:** DIN EN ISO 21675 or EPA Method 537.1 test protocols demonstrating compliance below statutory thresholds.

### Section 3: Design for Recycling (DfR) Assessment (Article 6 & Annex II)

All packaging must be recyclable. By 2030, packaging must meet specific **Design for Recycling (DfR)** criteria and be assigned a Recyclability Performance Grade.

#### 3.1 The Recyclability Grading Matrix (Annex II)
Packaging recyclability is rated based on its compatibility with standard sorting and recycling streams:

| Recyclability Grade | Recyclability Yield % | Status under PPWR |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Grade A** | **≥ 95%** | Highly recyclable; lowest EPR eco-modulation fee |
| **Grade B** | **≥ 80% to < 95%** | Recyclable; standard fee |
| **Grade C** | **≥ 70% to < 80%** | Conditionally acceptable; higher modulated fee |
| **Grade D / E** | **< 70%** | **PROHIBITED FROM 2030** (Deemed non-recyclable) |

#### 3.2 Required Evidence for Article 6
1. **Disruptor Evaluation:** Confirmation that inks, adhesives, barriers, or closures do not contaminate the main recycling stream (e.g., testing for water-soluble adhesives in paper or non-carbon black pigments in plastics for optical NIR sorter detectability).
2. **Recyclability Certificate:** Assessment report issued under recognized European design guidelines (e.g., RecyClass, CEFLEX, 4evergreen, or CITEO protocols).
3. **Recycling at Scale (from 2035):** Documentation demonstrating that the packaging material is collected, sorted, and recycled in installed infrastructure covering at least 75% of the EU population.

### Section 4: Minimum Post-Consumer Recycled Plastic Content (Article 7)

Where packaging includes plastic components, the technical dossier must verify the percentage of post-consumer recycled (PCR) plastic content.

#### 4.1 Statutory Targets (Phase 1: 1 January 2030)
* **30%** for contact-sensitive packaging made from polyethylene terephthalate (PET) as the major component (excluding single-use beverage bottles).
* **10%** for contact-sensitive packaging made from plastic materials other than PET.
* **30%** for single-use plastic beverage bottles.
* **35%** for all other plastic packaging.

#### 4.2 Evidentiary Standard
* Verified according to **EN 15343:2007** (Plastics recycling traceability and assessment of conformity and recycled content).
* Third-party chain of custody certification (e.g., ISCC PLUS, EuCertPlast, or RecyClass Recycling Process Certification) tracking material origin through a physical segregation or controlled blending mass-balance model.

### Section 5: Compostable Packaging Verification (Article 9 & Annex III)

Where packaging is claimed or mandated to be compostable:
1. **Mandatory Scope (from 12 February 2028):** Tea or coffee bags/systems/pods (Art. 3(1)(1)(f)) and sticky labels affixed to fruit and vegetables must be compatible with standards for industrial composting in bio-waste facilities (and home-composting where required by Member States).
2. **Technical Criteria (Annex III):** The dossier must demonstrate that the packaging does not cause contamination of compost or non-compostable waste streams, allows complete biological decomposition into $CO_2$, water, and mineral salts, and enters the organic waste stream.
3. **Evidentiary Standard:** Test certificates complying with **EN 13432:2000** (Packaging - Requirements for packaging recoverable through composting and biodegradation) or **EN 14995:2006** (Plastics - Evaluation of compostability).

### Section 6: Packaging Minimisation & Empty Space Audit (Article 10, Article 24 & Annex IV)

Packaging must be engineered to the minimum weight and volume necessary to ensure functionality, safety, and hygiene, without unnecessary marketing bulk.

#### 6.1 Article 10 & Annex IV Weight and Volume Limits
* The manufacturer must document that every structural feature (wall thickness, internal dimensions, headspace, tare weight) satisfies the performance criteria of **Annex IV, Part A** (product protection, packaging manufacturing processes, logistics, information presentation).
* **Prohibited Features:** Packaging with characteristics that aim only to increase perceived volume (double walls, false bottoms, unnecessary layers) is banned unless protected by registered Community designs or trademarks acquired before **11 February 2025** (Article 10(2)).

#### 6.2 Article 24 Maximum 50% Empty Space Ratio
* For grouped packaging, transport packaging, and **e-commerce packaging**, the empty space ratio must not exceed **50%**.
* Empty space ratio formula:
  $$\text{Empty Space Ratio} = \frac{V_{\text{outer}} - V_{\text{contents}}}{V_{\text{outer}}} \times 100$$
* *Statutory Rule on Fillers:* Space filled by filling materials (paper cuttings, air cushions, bubble wraps, foam chips) is legally classified as **empty space**.
* **Evidentiary File Record:** Volumetric measurements and CAD calculations demonstrating that void space remains $\le 50\%$.

---

### Section 7: Reusability Verification (Article 11 & Article 26, Annex VI)

If packaging is declared as **reusable**:
1. It must be designed to withstand a documented number of rotations under typical handling conditions (tested per **EN 13429:2004**).
2. It must be supported by an established, operational reconditioning and return system conforming to **Annex VI** (open-loop pool or closed-loop logistics system).
3. Reconditioning and maintenance protocols must be documented (cleaning, inspection, component replacement).

---

## 3. Statutory Retention & Archival Protocol

* **Single-Use Packaging:** The technical documentation and the corresponding DoC must be maintained for **5 years** after the packaging model was last placed on the market.
* **Reusable Packaging:** Must be maintained for **10 years** after the packaging was last placed on the market.
* **Format:** Secure digital repository accessible within 10 days of formal written demand from any EU Member State market surveillance authority.
